The painting happens on the raw biscuit, where the clay drinks the pigment the moment
the brush touches it. There is no correcting a line once it is down, which is why the
pattern is loose — it has to be made in one pass or not at all.

A clear glaze goes over the top and the plate is fired a second time. That seals the
cobalt under a hard surface, so the pattern will outlast the dishwasher by some
decades.
