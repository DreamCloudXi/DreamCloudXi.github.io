Thrown on the wheel in batches of eight or nine, of which six or seven usually survive
the kiln. The clay is a coarse local stoneware with enough grog in it to leave a faint
texture under the thumb.

Only the inside is glazed. That keeps the vessel watertight while leaving the outside
as the clay came off the wheel — sanded smooth after firing, but still recognisably
earth. It will pick up the marks of being used, which is the point.
