## What do they use every day?

The safest handmade gift is a replacement for something dull they already reach
for. A mug, a bowl, a lamp, a basket. The bar is not "would they buy this" but
"would they rather have this one than the one they have", and a hand-thrown mug
clears that bar against a supermarket mug almost every time.

## What would they never buy themselves?

The opposite tactic, and it works on people who are careful with money. Anything
they would describe as an indulgence — a print for a wall they have left blank
for four years, a candle they would never spend twenty dollars on — is a gift
precisely because they have already decided not to buy it.

## Is there a date on it?

Personalised work is a risk when it is decorative and a near-certainty when it
marks something: a first address, a wedding date, the year a business started.
The lettering is not the gift. The fact that somebody wrote it down is.

## How much space do they have?

This is the question people skip, and it is the one that decides whether a gift
is kept or quietly rehomed. Someone in a small flat does not want a large
object, however good it is. Small, useful and excellent beats large and
impressive in almost every home we ship to.
