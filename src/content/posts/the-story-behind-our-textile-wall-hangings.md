## It starts with the cord

Every hanging begins as undyed cotton cord, cut to length and hung from a wooden
rod. The length is worked out first, because a knot pulls the cord shorter and
there is no way to add it back.

## Knot by knot

Most of the pattern is a handful of knots repeated: the square knot, the spiral,
the double half hitch. What makes a piece look effortless is the tension, which is
felt rather than measured, and takes years to get even.

## Finishing

Once the knotting is done the fringe is combed out and trimmed by eye against a
straight edge. This is the part that decides whether a hanging hangs flat, and it
is done last because it cannot be undone.

## Why they differ

Cotton takes up moisture, hands tire, and a Tuesday is not a Friday. Two hangings
made from the same pattern will never quite match, which is the reason to buy one
and not the other.
