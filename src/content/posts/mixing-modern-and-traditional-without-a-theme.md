"Eclectic" is the word people use when a room has not worked out. The rooms that
do work usually have one thing holding them together, and it is rarely the period
of the furniture.

## Agree on material

An oak table, a willow basket, a terracotta lamp and a linen cushion will sit
together whatever century they are from, because they are all doing the same
thing with different raw material. Add one laminate surface and the argument
starts.

## Or agree on color

If the materials are various, hold the palette tight. Three colors across a
whole room is not limiting; it is what lets you put a 1970s lamp next to a pot
made last month.

## Or agree on scale

A row of objects roughly the same height reads as a set even when they have
nothing else in common. This is why a mantelpiece works and a windowsill usually
does not.

## Pick one, not three

The failure mode is trying to hold all three at once, which produces a room so
consistent it looks bought in a single afternoon. One thread of agreement is
enough. Everything else can differ, and the differences are what make it look
like somebody lives there.
